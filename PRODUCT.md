# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

delegated: Vite + React + TypeScript, chosen for a fast browser-based playtest harness with a pure rules core.

## Users

Two people sharing a screen while playtesting a prototype card game. They need to draft openly, secretly form battle lines, and review the exact resolution of each clash.

## Product Purpose

Norse Kode is a playable digital prototype for testing whether known-card drafting followed by secret formation creates a compelling Viking battle card-game loop. Success is making it fast to repeat skirmishes and easy to inspect surprising interactions.

## Positioning

Both players know exactly which five warriors the other controls; uncertainty lives in the ordered formation and hidden Blood Oath commitments, not hidden ownership.

## Operating Context

A pass-and-play browser harness supports War setup, open ten-card drafting, formation locking, simultaneous oath reveal, sequential clashes, skirmish tokens, and War completion. Debug inspection and a plain-language combat log are first-class playtest tools.

## Capabilities and Constraints

The prototype uses three weapon types, configurable chain bonuses, Bloodsworn, Shield Wall, Ravenfeeder, Berserker, Shield Maiden Vengeance, and Skald Responsive Song. God/Mythos cards are reserved and disabled by default. No deckbuilding, accounts, online multiplayer, campaign, resource system, or final art should be added in v0.1.

## Brand Commitments

Working title: NORSE KODE. The game should feel like a Viking battle remembered through saga and song while remaining as readable and tactile as a traditional deck of cards.

## Evidence on Hand

The supplied Prototype Specification v0.1 is the only product evidence. No final illustrations, hero designs, God effects, or production balance data are available.

## Product Principles

- Make the Draft → Predict → Form → Reveal → Resolve loop quick to repeat.
- Keep numbers meaningful; special abilities bend rules without replacing strength.
- Expose enough resolution detail to turn every surprising outcome into a playtest question.
- Keep unresolved design decisions in configuration or explicit placeholders.
